Nutri — eating well as a family

Eating well as a family: the camera counts the plate, an AI dietitian remembers each person’s targets, and the menu is built around real supermarket prices.

What it is

Calorie counters are usually built for one person and for typing: find the product in a database, pick a weight, repeat five times a day. People give up within a week. Here the input is one gesture: point the camera at the plate and the app recognises the food and counts it. A barcode on a package is read the same way.

The hard part is not the counting, it is the family. Everyone has their own targets, their own restrictions and their own dislikes, and a day’s menu has to add up for every one of them at once. That is what the model-driven dietitian does: it holds each profile and builds a menu that fits everyone’s targets — and the real prices of an actual supermarket chain, not an invented average.

Specifications: Flutter — one source tree gives both the Android app and a Windows desktop build. Food and barcode recognition through the camera, the model answering through Groq, synchronisation through n8n. The interface is in English, Spanish and Russian: not a nicety but part of the brief — three people in one kitchen do not have to share a language.

What proves it

Working, 25 tests, interface in English, Spanish and Russian.

  • Runs on Android and on Windows from the same codebase.
  • Prices come from a real supermarket chain, not from an invented average.

What is inside

  • Flutter (Android + Windows)
  • camera and barcode recognition
  • Groq
  • n8n sync
