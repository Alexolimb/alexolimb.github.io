Work with me

Two different conversations live here. An employer needs to know what I can do and how soon I can start; a client needs to know what they will get, when, and what happens after that. Pick the one you came for.

About me

I build three kinds of thing: sites and web consoles, applications for phone and desktop, and automation — scripted work that runs on a server and does the repetitive half of somebody's day. Next.js and React on the web, Flutter on the phone, Node and Python on the server, n8n for the wiring between them.

Handover is not the end of the job. What you get is the running thing — a link that opens or an installer that installs — plus the source, the access, and a written page on how it works and how to rebuild it from scratch. After that I stay reachable: I watch that it is still alive and fix it when it isn't. Freelance work usually falls apart at exactly this point, not during the build.

Remote, on CET, in English, Spanish or Russian. If an off-the-shelf tool solves your case faster and cheaper than custom work, I'll say so during the first conversation — before you pay for anything.

How I work

  1. A conversation, 20–30 minutesYou describe the job in your own words. I ask questions until nothing is left that the two of us could be picturing differently. Call or messages, whichever suits you.
  2. Scope and a price, in writingWhat is included, what is deliberately left out, which parts it breaks into, in what order they get built, what I need from you, and what it costs. All of it before the first line of code.
  3. Built in parts, each one shown workingEvery finished part reaches you as something you can open — a link or an installer, not a screenshot of progress. You see the direction in week two, while changing it is still cheap.
  4. Handover, and I stay reachableDeployed and working, with the source, the access, and a page describing how it is built, how to update it and how to rebuild it on an empty machine. After that I keep an eye on it and fix what breaks.

What it costs

I quote after we talk, in writing and together with the scope. Until the job is taken apart, any number is a guess: two people saying “I need a site” can mean four pages or a client area with logins, and those are not the same price. The conversation takes twenty minutes and commits you to nothing.
